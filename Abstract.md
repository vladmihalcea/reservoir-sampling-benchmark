# A Survey of Reservoir Sampling Techniques in Production Metrics Frameworks for Advanced Computing Infrastructures: Theory vs. Practice

V Mihalcea

Application Performance Monitoring (APM) systems in present-day computing infrastructures must compute quantile estimates such as the 95th or 99th percentile of response times over unbounded data streams while using bounded memory. Reservoir sampling algorithms provide the theoretical foundation for this task, yet there is limited evidence of how major production metrics frameworks actually align with the academic and scientific literature. This paper analyzes the source code of four widely deployed frameworks in advanced or regular computing infrastructures: Dropwizard Metrics, Micrometer, Prometheus, VictoriaMetrics, and classifies their quantile estimation strategies according to the weighted reservoir sampling taxonomy proposed by [Hubschle-Schneider](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3549934). For each framework, we benchmark quantile accuracy and memory consumption using synthetic and realistic workload traces derived from database connection pool monitoring and HTTP request latency data. Moreover, we identify concrete suboptimalities: Dropwizard employs a single-threaded [forward-decay priority-sampling](http://dimacs.rutgers.edu/~graham/pubs/papers/fwddecay.pdf) implementation, whereas batched reservoir sampling achieves an order-of-magnitude speedup; its forward-decay reservoir hardcodes a decay parameter that has never been validated across diverse workload profiles; and VictoriaMetrics bypasses reservoir sampling entirely in favor of histogram bucket aggregation. The proposed results provide research and industry practitioners alike with an evidence-based guide for selecting or customizing sampling strategies in production metrics frameworks for advanced computing infrastructures and also highlight open research gaps in adaptive, tail-latency-preserving reservoir designs for critical infrastructure performance
